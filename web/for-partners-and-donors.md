# Tangent: an unconventional incubator for entrepreneurs

Tangent is a free, not-for-profit incubator in Singapore for underserved potential entrepreneurs — we are especially interested in incubating young people 18-30 but open to participants up to the age of 35. 

Tangent isn’t a venture capital incubator; instead we are the complement to venture capital. Venture capital exists to fund businesses that aim to become “unicorns” (i.e., very large companies) by building hypothetical enormous markets and/or new technologies, both of which entail great uncertainty. Tangent looks for the inverse: businesses that want to solve real problems in existing markets using well-understood technology, and which are okay with remaining small. 

Two things matter most for Tangent: 

1. Supporting founders who explicitly want to build businesses that get to free cash flow fast, ideally in under 12 months. This rules out a) inventing highly uncertain new technologies, b) building large new markets that don’t exist yet, c) accepting long periods with bad/negative unit economics, and usually d) building unicorns.
2. Supporting founders who are open to building their businesses informed by technology and AI, even if they aren’t building AI or tech products and services. 

Tangent’s unusual non-VC approach allows us to support a much more diverse range of entrepreneurship types: for-profit companies, social enterprises, not-for-profits, creative practices.

## Tangent programme details

Tangent is a four-week early stage startup sprint. Each participant gets a S$1,000 stipend, tools, a place to work in person, and guides from industry that we match specifically to what each team needs. There is no fixed curriculum and no mandatory assignments. Tangent takes no equity, and participants leave at the end of the four weeks owing nothing. (Those who want to continue after the four-week programme can pitch for additional funding from a separate, likely for-profit entity; the structure of this additional funding is still under development.)

We select into Tangent based on motivation and self-direction rather than grades or credentials. Tangent recruits from across the range of IHLs in Singapore, but is especially interested in having more participants from polytechnics and ITE. [Cohort 1](https://tangent.org.sg/cohort1) ran from 13 July to 7 August 2026 with 17 participants across 10 projects. [Cohort 2](https://tangent.org.sg/cohort-2-landing-page) will run 31 August to 25 September, and [Cohort 3](https://tangent.org.sg/cohort-3-landing-page) (space still available) from 19 October to 13 November. We also have five more cohorts scheduled through the end of 2027 (including one focused on creative industry participants in June 2027, in collaboration with Tusitala), each scheduled to coincide with IHL term breaks. 

## Organisational form
Tangent is the operating name of Go Tangent Ltd (UEN 201941957R), a Singapore prosocial company limited by guarantee. Its startup funding comes from private philanthropic contributions through the Majurity Trust; it takes no government funding at the moment. # The support we’re looking for

## Space for running the programme

Tangent has been given temporary space until end-October 2026, and is currently looking for a more permanent home thereafter.

- **What we need:** 1100 square feet runs the programme. 2000-2500 square feet of separated and securable space would let a community of Tangent alumni and the wider SME entrepreneurship community form around it.
- **What we can pay:** as a not-for-profit, Tangent is looking for discounted or sponsored space.
- **What a host gets back:** programming and placemaking, not just occupancy. Tangent already runs workshops on writing product requirements, building a business model from first principles rather than a template, developing a narrative that makes sense to a customer, and asking better business model questions.
- **In a space large enough:** those workshops, and drop-in coworking for alumni and other young founders, open to the wider community using the facility on a space-available basis.
- **If the host runs its own entrepreneurship programming:** Tangent could co-run sessions for that partner’s audience, and the partner could send aspiring entrepreneurs to Tangent.
- **What Tangent brings:** its own staff, tooling, and framework. What a host provides is space and a community to share it with.

## Funding

Tangent’s current funding covers three of the eight cohorts scheduled through the end of 2027. We’re looking for funding for overheads and cohort operating expenses for the remaining scheduled cohorts. Contributions will go directly to running the programme.

- **Per-cohort operating expenses:** participant stipends, costs of tools and software subscriptions provided to teams, food and beverage for cohort events.
- **Overheads:** salaries for the 2 Tangent staff, plus general and admin expenses.
- **Tax treatment:** we may have a mechanism for making funding contributions tax-deductible, but this is still TBD.

## Operating cohort runs for institutions

Tangent would also like to explore generating revenue to fund our programmes by  operating paid Tangent-model cohorts for institutions.

- **Who for:** a polytechnic, a university, a foundation, or a company that wants a four-week programme for its own students, members, or staff.
- **What it is:** the same programme, with the same plan-of-work process, guides, workshops, stipend and tooling, run for a group the institution brings.
- **Terms:** fee-for-service, to be negotiated

## Referrals

The people Tangent is looking for are already building things without being asked to. They have a problem they keep coming back to, they are self-directed, and they are often not the most conventionally impressive person on paper. No technical background is needed, and the idea does not have to be a company in the ordinary sense. If you work with young people in Singapore and one or two of them fit that description, send them to tangent.org.sg, or refer them directly to us.

## Guides

The most useful thing many people can give Tangent is two hours. Guides come in for a single unstructured session with participants who are working on something specific and are stuck on something specific. There is nothing to prepare and no lecture to give: the participants bring the problem. What makes a guide valuable is industry experience and domain knowledge, which usually means having built or sold something inside a real vertical, run operations in one, or sat on the buying side of the decision a participant is trying to understand. Tangent matches guides to teams by what each team actually needs rather than keeping a general pool, and a guide who wants to keep going with a team can choose to do so (but is not obligated to do so). If you or someone in your organisation would be willing to spend two hours with a young founder, start at [tangent.org.sg/guide](https://tangent.org.sg/guide).
